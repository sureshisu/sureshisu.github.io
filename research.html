Research

My research sits at the intersection of computational fluid dynamics, electromagnetism, and scientific machine learning. I develop numerical solvers and data-driven models for fluids that interact with electromagnetic fields, with applications ranging from fusion energy to plasma physics, and I use high-performance computing to push these simulations to realistic scales.

Current work at the Translational AI Center focuses on variational multiscale finite element methods for turbulent and multiphase flows, neural-network closures learned directly from experimental data, and operator-learning surrogates for time-dependent flow over complex geometries. Earlier work at Virginia Tech developed Maxwell–Navier–Stokes solvers for liquid metal flows in fusion liquid walls.

Research Projects

Oct 2025
Translational AI Center

Turbulent Flow Past an Airfoil

  • Simulated 3D turbulent flow over a NACA0012 airfoil at a chord Reynolds number of 6 million using a variational multiscale (VMS) finite element framework.
  • Used the VMS formulation to separate resolved and subgrid scales, enabling turbulence modeling through variational projection without empirical closure models.
  • Obtained surface pressure and flow structures in good agreement with NASA CFL3D data, capturing coherent vortical features near the airfoil surface.
Oct 2025
Translational AI Center

Droplet Dynamics in Non-Newtonian Fluids

  • Used a Cahn–Hilliard–Navier–Stokes (CHNS) framework to simulate how a non-Newtonian DLP (digital light processing) resin rises through another immiscible fluid.
  • Trained a neural network to learn the viscosity–shear-rate relationship directly from data, with Lipschitz regularization to suppress overfitting.
  • Employed octree-based adaptive mesh refinement within the finite element framework to efficiently capture interface dynamics.
Dec 2023
Virginia Tech

Liquid Metal Dynamics in Fusion Liquid Walls

  • Developed a novel Maxwell–Navier–Stokes solver, written in C, for single- and two-phase flow problems in the presence of electromagnetic fields.
  • Reformulated Maxwell's equations in their potential form to accurately represent the fusion liquid wall under study.
  • Incorporated the skin effect due to eddy currents, enabling precise representation of fluid behavior in unsteady electromagnetic conditions.
Dec 2018
Virginia Tech

Transverse Magnetic Fields and Electrically Conducting Liquids

  • Developed a custom OpenFOAM solver to investigate the effects of transverse magnetic fields on the flow of electrically conducting liquids, using the electric potential formulation of magnetohydrodynamics.
  • Assessed how distributed Lorentz forces alter velocity and pressure distributions.
  • Conducted a parametric study to evaluate the impact of the Hartmann number on flow behavior.
